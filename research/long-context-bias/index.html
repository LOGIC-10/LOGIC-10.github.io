Findings of ACL 2025 / Conference paper

Distance between Relevant Information Pieces Causes Bias in Long-Context LLMs

Studying how the spacing between related facts changes a language model’s ability to use a long context.

Runchu Tian*, Yanghao Li*, Yuepeng Fu, Siyang Deng, Qinyu Luo, Cheng Qian, Shuo Wang, Xin Cong, Zhong Zhang, Yesai Wu, Yankai Lin, Huadong Wang, Xiaojiang Liu

* Equal contribution.

01 / The question

Does the distance between relevant facts matter, beyond their individual positions in a long input?

02 / The approach

Long-context positional bias

LongPiBench varies the placement and spacing of multiple relevant information pieces across tasks and input lengths. The study distinguishes absolute position from relative distance to reveal biases that single-fact retrieval tests can miss.
